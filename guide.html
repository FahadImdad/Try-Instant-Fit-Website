📖 User Guide

How Try Instant Fit Works

Everything a shop needs to set it up and run it — and everything a customer needs to try a garment on. Pick your side below.

What you're setting up

You upload a photo of a garment. We turn it into a QR code you print or display in your shop. A customer scans it, adds a photo of themselves, and sees themselves wearing that exact garment — then shares or downloads the result. You see every try-on, every lead, and every credit spent in your dashboard.

What it does and doesn't do. Try Instant Fit places the garment photo you uploaded onto the customer. It does not invent fabric, restyle a design, or change colours. Your design appears exactly as you shot it — so upload the image you want customers to see.

Setting up your brand

Five steps, start to finish. Steps 1–3 get your first QR code live; 4 and 5 are how you control access and cost once customers start scanning.

  1. 1

    Register your brand /register

    Sign up with the details customers will see on the try-on page and on your printed QR codes.

    • Brand name — as customers know it, e.g. Khaadi, Sana Safinaz
    • Your name and position — who we contact about the account
    • WhatsApp number — where customer leads reach you, e.g. +92 300 1234567
    • Country and website URL
    • Brand logo and brand colour — these brand the customer's try-on page and their downloaded image

    Upload a clean logo here even if you're in a hurry. It's what appears on every image a customer saves and shares from your shop.

  2. 2

    Add your products Dashboard → Products & QRs

    Each product is one garment photo plus its details. Press Add Product and fill in:

    • Product image (required) — JPEG, PNG or WebP
    • Name (required) — e.g. Black Cotton Kurta
    • Price and currency, with an option to show the price on the downloaded QR
    • Description — material, fit, colour, sizes
    • Available sizes

    The garment photo decides the result quality. Shoot the piece flat or on a plain background, evenly lit, with the whole garment in frame and no crop at the hem or sleeves. A crisp, well-lit product shot is the single biggest factor in how good every try-on from that QR looks.

  3. 3

    Generate a QR code Products & QRs → Generate a QR Code

    Pick an existing product, choose how it should be gated, and download the printable QR.

    • Pick a product — the garment this QR tries on
    • Require a passcode to scan this QR — on for staff-controlled access, off for open scanning
    • Free/open try-on cap — required when the passcode is off. The number of try-ons allowed before the QR locks itself
    • Expiry date — optional; after it, the QR stops working

    Then Customize your printed QR — your logo, brand colour and optional price are baked into the downloadable image. Print it for a shelf talker, hang tag, window sticker, or show it on a tablet at the counter.

  4. 4

    Control access with passcodes Products & QRs → Passcodes

    Passcodes are how you keep try-ons for real customers instead of passers-by. They are brand-wide: any active passcode of yours works on any of your passcode-gated QR codes.

    • Code — type your own, e.g. SARAH50, or auto-generate one
    • Use limit — how many try-ons that code allows in total
    • Expiry — optional cut-off date
    • Customer name — optional label so you know who you gave it to

    Codes are matched in capitals, so sarah50 and SARAH50 are the same code. Print them in capitals to save confusion at the counter.

  5. 5

    Keep credits topped up Dashboard → Top Up Credits

    One completed try-on uses one credit. Credits are deducted only after a try-on succeeds — a failed or abandoned attempt costs you nothing.

    • Preset bundles of 100, 500, 1,000 or 5,000 try-ons, or enter a custom number
    • Your rate per try-on is shown live as you pick the amount, and your spend to date sits in Analytics
    • Past requests are listed under Top-up history

    Run out and every QR stops. Customers see: “This store has run out of try-on credits.” Credits are checked before the customer uploads a photo, so nobody wastes effort — but it's a bad look on the shop floor. Top up while you still have headroom.

How a QR decides who gets in

Four gates are checked every time someone scans, in this order. Knowing them makes counter problems obvious in seconds.

CheckResultWhat the customer sees
Your credits Blocks “This store has run out of try-on credits.” Top up to clear it.
QR active & not expired Blocks “This QR has expired.” or “This QR isn't active.” Generate a fresh one.
Free cap reached Switches The QR locks itself and starts asking for a passcode — it does not die. Hand out a passcode and the same printed QR keeps working.
Passcode valid Blocks Invalid, expired, or all uses spent. Issue a new code from Passcodes.

The one behaviour to remember. An open QR that hits its free cap doesn't stop working — it converts to passcode-only, permanently. That's your anti-abuse valve: print once, keep it on the shelf forever, and control access with codes from then on.

Running it well on the shop floor

  • Brief your staff on one sentence: “Scan this, add your photo, see it on yourself.” That's the whole pitch — no training needed.
  • Place QRs where people hesitate — the fitting-room queue, a shelf of a size that's out of stock, the counter while they wait.
  • Use passcodes as a reason to talk. Handing over a code is a natural opening, and it ties the try-on to a named customer in Leads.
  • Re-shoot weak product photos. If try-ons from one QR look worse than others, the garment image is almost always why.
  • Check credits before a sale weekend, not during it.

Getting a good result

The app is only as good as the photo you give it. One decent photo beats five rushed ones.

  • Stand against a plain background — a blank wall works best.
  • Full body, facing forward, with your head and feet in frame.
  • Good even light. Face a window; avoid strong shadows or backlight.
  • Wear something close-fitting. Bulky coats confuse the fit.
  • Arms slightly away from your sides, so your outline is clear.

Not happy with the result? Press Try Another Photo and use a clearer one. Most disappointing try-ons are a cropped, dim or angled photo — not the garment.

What happens to your photo

Your photo is never saved on our servers. It's used only to create your preview, and the result isn't stored either — download it if you want to keep it, because it won't be waiting for you later. Full detail is in our shopper privacy notice.
